Every seller asks the same question: should I fix this first? The answer depends on which of three groups the job falls into.

## Usually worth doing

These are cheap, quick and change how the home shows. More than half of agents surveyed by the National Association of Realtors in 2025 recommend them before listing:

- Decluttering and a full-home clean
- Paint touch-ups, or repainting bold rooms in a neutral colour
- Minor repairs: taps, handles, caulking, cracked tiles
- Carpet cleaning and re-grouting tile
- Curb appeal: lawn, garden beds, front door
- Professional photos

## Worth doing if an inspector will flag it

Most buyers make their offer conditional on a home inspection, or inspect before offering. Problems an inspector will find are better handled on your terms than argued over during negotiation:

- A roof at the end of its life
- A furnace or air conditioner that is not working properly
- Active leaks, or stains from old ones
- Electrical problems, such as an overloaded panel
- Wet basements

You do not always have to fix these. You can also price the home to reflect them, or get a quote so buyers see a real number in place of their own worst guess. We can help you choose.

## Rarely worth doing

- **Full kitchen or bathroom renovations.** You seldom recover the whole cost, and your taste may not be the buyer's.
- **Finishing a basement.** Expensive, slow, and it needs permits to be done properly.
- **New windows throughout.** Buyers value them less than they cost.
- **Anything that delays listing into a weaker season.**

## What you must tell buyers

Ontario follows "buyer beware" for defects a buyer or their inspector could reasonably spot. Hidden defects are different. According to the Real Estate Council of Ontario, a seller must disclose latent defects: problems that are not apparent on a reasonable inspection and make the home dangerous or unfit to live in. Hiding one can lead to a lawsuit after closing.

If you know about something serious, such as a foundation problem or past flooding, tell your agent early. If you are not sure whether it counts, ask a real estate lawyer before you list.

## A simple way to decide

For any repair, ask three things:

1. Will a buyer see it in the photos or in the first five minutes?
2. Will a home inspector flag it?
3. Does it cost less than the discount a buyer would ask for?

Two yeses usually means do it. If you would like a second opinion, we will walk through your home and give you a short list.
